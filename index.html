Black influence on contemporary indie music: a selection

For this online exhibit, I've chosen to pair together major indie music artists with their core influences, spanning multiple genres from techno to trip hop. This selection is meant to expose the viewer to the ubiquity of black influence in today's most pervasive subgenres, and the different means in which it pervades them.

After visiting “The Music is Black: A British Story” exhibit at the V&A East Museum, I was inspired by its storytelling and organization (sectioning off of genres, chronology, etc) and interplay of influences. I was also particularly interested in the synesthesia of the experience: as one walked through the exhibit, sensored headphones would play different songs depending on the objects being viewed. At times though, I found myself wishing I could listen to and have a bit more control over the music, as I felt that would be more informative than prescriptive. I wanted to recreate this experience online, so that anyone with a device could experience it from anywhere with internet access. While I appreciated the exclusivity of the exhibit, as it can protect the artifacts themselves and work against commodification, I wanted to make something more accessible and show that art or something to the token of “high art” (not at all to toot my own horn, rather to just make a case against restriction to curated art) doesn’t need to be exclusive.

The full exhibit features found images, online displays of artifacts such as books, displays of song lyrics, and more, as well as embedded songs meant to be listened to as part of the exhibition experience. I picked six different sub-exhibits, each featuring pioneers of different facets of indie music, as well as their influences from pioneers of other genres headed by black individuals and the black diaspora. However, these two are definitely not mutually exclusive, as shown here through the Trip Hop exhibit: how a new genre emerged, headed by black pioneers. The exhibition itself is intentionally surface-level, meant to give a short introduction to each artist and genre, and to touch upon multiple of those genres. If you’re curious about a specific sub-exhibit, I’d be happy to provide more sources and information.

Hope you enjoy! Please visit the “About” page for more background and to provide feedback!
